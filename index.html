Nine palettes, two live examples each. One of them was pulled from a photo of twin towers in the sun. Plain HTML, CSS and vanilla JS. No libraries, no outside requests.

Looking up

Glass facade

Why it fits: the photo is a wall of tilted glass, so the page rebuilds it: five values scattered as lit and unlit windows, with a band of sun sweeping across.

Demonstrates: CSS grid generated from JS, a rotated layer, mix-blend-mode, and an IntersectionObserver that stops the flicker off-screen.

#06111F

Shadow to sun scrubber

Why it fits: the brief was a gradient of sky and sun. Drag from the deepest teal to the washed-out sky and the stage becomes whatever the gradient is at that point.

Demonstrates: colour interpolation in plain JS, a custom property driving the background, and a live linear-gradient() string ready to copy.

Horizon

Why it fits: this palette runs from near-black navy to a pale sky, so the sun sits exactly where the darkest and lightest values meet.

Demonstrates: hard colour stops for a horizon line, a radial-gradient sun with mix-blend-mode: screen, and a masked repeating gradient for the water glint.

    Field notes
    On a clear day the towers hold the whole sky in their glass, and the city stops looking like steel and starts looking like weather. Nothing here is loud. The colours sit close together and let the light do the work.
    A muted palette earns its keep in text. It is calm enough to read for a long time and still has a pulse of teal when something needs to stand out.
    Quiet colours, one good accent.
    

    Editorial column
    Why it fits: hazy, low-contrast colours feel like a magazine page, so the palette sets type instead of decorating it.
    Demonstrates: CSS multi-column layout, a ::first-letter drop cap, text-wrap: balance and pretty, and break-inside: avoid.
    Glow is on.
    

    Settings panel
    Why it fits: muted colours make controls feel calm. Flipping a switch changes the whole panel without any script.
    Demonstrates: restyled native checkboxes with appearance: none, the :has() selector reacting to state, and accent-color on the range.
